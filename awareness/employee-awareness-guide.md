# Employee Awareness Guide: Recognising and Reporting Phishing

## What is phishing?
Phishing is a social-engineering attack in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organisation to persuade someone to reveal information, transfer money, open a malicious attachment or visit a fraudulent website.

## Warning signs
- An unexpected request for passwords, payment, personal data or MFA codes.
- A sender address or reply-to address that does not match the organisation.
- A display name that looks familiar but uses an unusual email domain.
- Urgent or threatening language designed to bypass normal checks.
- Links whose destination does not match the displayed text.
- Unexpected attachments or requests to enable macros.
- Unusual tone, formatting, spelling or context.

## Before you act
1. Pause. Do not let urgency override normal procedures.
2. Check the complete sender address, not just the display name.
3. Verify unusual requests using a known contact method, not the details in the email.
4. Preview links carefully; do not visit suspicious destinations.
5. Do not open unexpected attachments or enable macros.
6. Never share passwords or MFA codes by email or through a link.
7. Report suspicious messages using your organisation's approved process.

## If you clicked or disclosed information
- Contact your IT/security team immediately.
- Follow organisational instructions for password resets and session revocation.
- Report any information disclosed, files opened or actions taken.
- Do not delete the message unless the security team instructs you to do so; it may be useful evidence.

## Remember
A legitimate-looking logo, HTTPS padlock or familiar name does not prove an email is safe. Verify the request and the sender through a trusted channel.
